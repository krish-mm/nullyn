Nullyn AI: low-latency feedback suppression

Full draft available under NDA on request — the complete paper is unpublished

The draft paper, Toward Low-Latency Neural Feedback Suppression for Live Music, reviews the published evidence and sets out how Nullyn AI will be measured. It has not been peer reviewed, and Nullyn AI’s own results have not been reported yet. The full draft is unpublished and available under NDA on request.

Abstract

Acoustic feedback — the howl when a microphone picks up its own loudspeaker — limits how loud a live sound system can run. Fifty years of research has produced three main families of automatic control: frequency shifting and phase modulation, notch-filter howling suppression, and adaptive feedback cancellation. In the field, many sound engineers still prefer to control feedback by hand because automatic systems are not reliable enough. Since 2022, neural networks have been applied to howling suppression; the studies reviewed for this paper evaluate on speech, mostly with simulated loop delays of 100 to 500 ms, and most report speech-separation scores rather than the stable-gain measures the feedback field uses.

Live music is different. Instruments and sung notes are tonal, and that is exactly what classical howl detectors confuse with feedback. Performers on in-ear monitors can notice added delay of a few milliseconds. This paper sets out that evidence, names the gaps a live-music system has to close, and describes Nullyn AI at a high level: a closed-loop system that listens to the microphone and to a reference of the speaker feed, then cancels feedback, with an AI notch as fallback. Nullyn AI’s own results will be added when they are produced. Method detail is in the full draft, available under NDA on request.
